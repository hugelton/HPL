# Legal and implementation considerations

HPL 1.0 is published for adoption by rightsholders. This document records nonbinding considerations for adopters; it is not legal advice or a determination of enforceability. The LICENSE text controls.

- Confirm whether advertisement-funded hosting, paid hardware bundles and distribution within corporate groups are intended to be treated as drafted. A free software mirror with unrelated advertising should not inadvertently be restricted.
- Clarify the boundary between paid customization and disguised software sales, and between independently sold plugins and monetized feature gates.
- Consider whether the network-source requirement should cover unmodified external deployments as well. Version 1.0 covers modified versions, including free hosting, and is stronger than MPL in this respect.
- Review copyright versus contract enforceability of service restrictions in Japan and target jurisdictions, statutory exceptions, assent, consumer protections, patent grant and termination provisions. Consider explicit contract acceptance for hosted or commercial arrangements.
- Decide whether a fixed three-year source availability obligation is appropriate, particularly for small distributors.
- Review the scope of control for affiliated entities and contractor access.
- Confirm all relevant copyright and patent rights before relicensing Nerikone. Previously granted Apache-2.0 rights remain available to recipients. Preserve third-party notices and license files; review compatibility with copyleft dependencies individually.
- Establish an authorized licensing contact and optionally a separate contributor agreement granting commercial relicensing authority. An ordinary contribution under HPL does not establish such authority. Do not infer it from a DCO or a pull request.
- Check the name and abbreviation for conflicts and seek qualified legal review where practicable, particularly before commercial licensing or enforcement. Use a custom SPDX LicenseRef; do not claim official SPDX listing or OSI approval.

## Unified material scope

- Review whether product commercialization restrictions can be enforced through copyright, patents, design rights or contractual assent in Japan and other jurisdictions. A file notice alone may not form a contract with every downstream manufacturer or purchaser.
- Confirm that at-cost transfers of physical kits require permission while genuine contract manufacturing for internal use remains permitted.
- Define covered paths explicitly for mixed-license KiCad projects, including libraries, BOM and fabrication files. Do not imply rights over standard footprints or third-party assets.
- Check whether the source requirement can be met for proprietary CAD tools and licensed component libraries without infringing third-party rights.
- Consider regulatory compliance, product safety and mandatory product liability independently of the license disclaimer.
- Publication of the license does not establish that every restriction is enforceable in every jurisdiction.
